## Scene 1 — The Salty Dog Tavern

**Read aloud (opening):**

> Harbor's Rest, Driftwood Isle. Morning fog is peeling off the water and the whole town smells of brine, woodsmoke, and cheap ale. Through the window: the pier, the market square, and — moored at the end of the pier, hull painted black with a gilded anchor — an Iron Fleet patrol ship. It's been here three days. Nobody says why. Nobody wants to ask.
> 
> The Salty Dog is the only tavern in town worth sitting in. A one-legged former sailor named **Brin** runs the bar. The notice board by the door is half crew jobs, half wanted posters. The Iron Fleet's tribute collector was last seen here yesterday, and everyone in this room is acting like they didn't see him.

**NPCs in the room:**

- **Brin** (barkeep) — missing a leg, wary, keeps glancing at the door. Knows everyone's business.
- **Old Tom** (dockhand) — grumbling about tribute. Can confirm the patrol ship's been "asking questions" about who sails out of this port.
- **Kael** (fisherman, hunched in the corner) — his daughter **Lyra** was taken by Iron Fleet sailors on a "stolen nets" charge. When players try to talk to him he is too scared to say anything, except if he is pressed (intimidation or persuasion) he talks. He'll offer his fishing net (the bribe item from Scene 2) if they promise to get Lyra back.
- **Notice board:** "Sailor needed for coastal run — pay on delivery," a wanted poster for a Red Legion smuggler, and a faded Free Captains recruitment flyer.

**The hook:** Brin slides a tankard over and drops his voice:

> _"The Black Brigades took a fisherman's daughter for 'stealing nets.' Holding her at the docks. If you can get her back, I'll recommend you to a good captain."_

**Player check-in (table talk, not a roll):** each player states why they're in Harbor's Rest and why they want to get on a ship. Brin, Old Tom, and the notice board are all legitimate answers to "who do I sign with?" — the session answers that question for them.

**Gathering intel before they leave (optional beats, not required):**

- **Old Tom confirms the charge.** A DC 8 Investigation, or simply asking the right question, and he'll lean in: the patrol ship has been asking specifically about **Kael's nets**, and the "charge" was signed by a lieutenant in person. This makes the docks scene feel connected, not random.
- **Kael in the corner.** If players approach him, he's terrified and won't speak freely. A DC 10 Persuasion (or Intimidation) gets him to talk. In exchange for a promise to get Lyra back, he presses his **fishing net** into their hands — "they'll take it, they always take something." This is the pre-built bribe for Scene 2's Negotiate option, and it turns Kael from a plot device into a person.
- **The notice-board job.** The "coastal run — pay on delivery" poster is real: a merchant wants cargo escorted past the outer shoals. Picking it up is a legitimate side-thread and a reason the Iron Fleet is watching the docks. Ignoring it is fine — it's a hook for later, not a gate.

**The "do nothing" branch.** If the players have no interest in Lyra, the session does not collapse. Brin still recommends them to a captain via the notice-board job or Old Tom's contact. The Lyra rescue is the *strongest* path to Elias, but not the only door.

## Scene 1.5 — The Walk to the Docks

**Read aloud (transition):**

> The street between the tavern and the docks is narrow, cobbled, slick with the morning tide. A market square: a fishmonger with a scale and a knife, a rope seller, a woman haggling over salted cod. Every few feet, the fog thickens. From the pier you can hear the slow grind of an anchor chain — the black-hulled patrol ship is still at the end of the pier, and one of its sailors is on the quay, talking to the fishmonger.

**A single optional complication:** the Fleet sailor is "helping himself" to a basket of fish on the grounds of a "shortage." Players can:

- **Step in** — DC 10 Persuasion or Intimidation to de-escalate. Success earns the fishmonger's gratitude and a quiet rumor: *"That lieutenant's been here three days, and he's not just collecting. Ask anyone who hasn't lost something yet."* Failure means the sailor takes the fish and **memorizes their faces** (a seed for later).
- **Walk past** — no cost, but the sailor watches them go, and the fishmonger's face closes like a door.

Either way, the scene ends with the players at the edge of the dock, the camp ahead, and the sound of the anchor chain fading as the patrol ship's gangplank is raised.

## Scene 2 — The Docks: Iron Fleet Ambush

**The dockside camp** (2–3 blocks from the tavern, visible from the market square): a canvas tent, crates stacked as a barricade, **two sailors** standing over **Lyra** (bound, sitting on a crate — she's scared, not hurt), and **one sailor** patrolling the crate line.

**How it plays out — dynamic, pick whichever the players do:**

|Option|Check|Success|Failure|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Stealth**|DC 12 Perception/Stealth, 30-second window (sailors bickering over stolen fish)|Slip past, untie Lyra, leave with no one the wiser|A patrol sailor spots them — he shouts _"Fleet! We've got stowaways!"_ and combat starts|
|**Distract**|DC 10 Thievery to knock a crate / start a fire|Sailors rush the noise; players grab Lyra in the chaos, no fight|They trigger the alarm anyway, but the sailors are already turning — combat|
|**Negotiate**|DC 10 Persuasion, bribe with Kael's fishing net (or coin)|Sailors let Lyra go, take the "bribe," grumble about their pay|One sailor reports it — the whole camp stands up|

**A secondary objective — the tribute ledger:** through the canvas tent's flap, visible on a crate, is a **tribute ledger** — the Iron Fleet's records of what they've "collected" from Harbor's Rest this month. It's not just fish. Names, families, payments. Grabbing it (DC 12 Sleight of Hand while the sailors are distracted, or as a bonus action during combat) is **evidence of corruption** they can use later, and it gives a reason to succeed beyond "save the girl."

**If it goes to combat:** 2–4 sailors (see stat block below). They fight dirty — shout for backup, aim for the face, try to knock the players down and bind them.

**Environmental options:**
- DC 12 Athletics to topple the crate barricade, knocking a sailor prone.
- The dock is 10 ft above the water — DC 12 Acrobatics to slide off the edge and re-approach from below (the sailors can't follow without falling in).
- A barrel of oil near the barricade — DC 12 Thievery to light it, creating a fire line.

**Failure consequences (if all options fail):** the patrol sailor's shout carries. Two more sailors run from the market square. **The clock is now 2 rounds before Rourke's reinforcements arrive.** This creates urgency and justifies Rourke's entrance even if the players somehow hold their own.

---

### Rourke Steel enters

**This is the set piece.** No matter how well the players are doing, **Lieutenant Rourke Steel** arrives — he's been watching the dock all morning. He's CR 6 and _far_ out of their league. The moment he shows up, the tone shifts:

> The sailors' shouting dies. A heavy boot crunches on the dock planks. Out of the fog steps a tall man in full plate, a gilded anchor gleaming on his breastplate, a war hammer dragging behind him. His men fall silent. Even Lyra stops crying.
> 
> **Rourke** _(calm, almost bored)_: "So this is the 'thief' problem. I was hoping I wouldn't have to spend my morning on it."

**Rourke's approach:** He doesn't rush in. He uses **Iron Fleet Authority** and **Command** to freeze the players, then offers a choice:

> "Kneel, and I'll take you in for questioning. Stand, and I'll break you and take you in for _execution_. I'm a reasonable man."

**If they kneel (the surrender path):** Rourke takes them to the patrol ship.
- DC 15 Deception to convince the guard they're "local dockhands, not pirates." Success: held for one hour in a cell — the sound of the anchor chain, a chance to plan an escape. Failure: Rourke interrogates them." They're released with a warning: *"Next time I see you, it's the Maw."* Elias still saves them, but the tone is "narrow escape" rather than "instant rescue."

**If they negotiate (the evidence path):** with the **tribute ledger** in hand, a DC 15 Persuasion (or DC 18 without it) to prove the "stolen nets" charge is fabricated. Success: Rourke is _stunned_. He doesn't back down, but he hesitates — and that hesitation is the window Elias walks through. This is a non-combat path that still lands on Elias's entrance.

**The fight (if they stand):** Rourke is a wall. He'll parry, command his men to flank, and keep trying to talk them into surrendering. If they keep hitting him, he gets angry — and he _crushes mutinies with his bare hands_. **He will beat them down.** This is the moment the players should be losing badly (below ~50% HP, or downed).

> **DM note:** Don't let Rourke kill them. He's here to _break_ them, not finish them. The scene's job is to make them feel helpless so the rescue lands.

---

### Elias's rescue

**Trigger:** The players are losing — below ~50% HP, or one is down.

> A low whistle cuts through the fog — someone's whistling the Iron Fleet's own march, _slightly_ off-key, mocking. Then a voice, calm and close, like it's right behind you:
> 
> **"The Fleet's claim on you just expired. Stand up."**
> 
> A figure steps out of the mist — lean, weathered, wearing a captain's coat over a sailor's rig, and something at his throat that glints like a compass. He doesn't draw a weapon. He doesn't need to.

**Elias**: "Long time no see, Rourke."

**Rourke _(grinding his teeth)_**: "Veyne!"

**Elias**: "Didn't I tell you last time you shouldn't make up charges as an excuse to kidnap girls? What is it this time — stolen fish?"

**Rourke**: "I don't make up charges, and she _stole_ fishing nets!"

**Elias**: "Fishing nets? Really. You should come up with something better. Nobody would believe you're important enough to go after net thieves. Let them walk away now, or we'll settle this properly — and you know how this would end."

**Rourke** _(glancing at his men, jaw tight)_: "Fine. But don't think this is over, Veyne."

**The small action beat:** as Rourke turns to leave, one of his sailors raises a blade toward a still-downed player. Elias's hand moves — not to draw a weapon, but to **catch the sailor's wrist**. A moment of stillness. Then he lets go. *"Don't."* The sailor lowers the blade. This shows he's capable of violence without committing to it, and makes the "I don't take prisoners" line land harder.

**The personal threat:** Rourke's last line is aimed at one specific player, not the group: *"You. I'll remember your face. The Fleet doesn't forget."*

He turns and walks off with his sailors. The dock goes quiet. Lyra is released. The players are staring at the stranger who just saved their lives.

## Scene 3 — The Hook: Getting on the Ship

**Elias, now that the coast is clear.** He's not smug. He's tired. He looks at the players — at their bruises, at Lyra — and he's assessing them.

> "You've got a choice. You can go back to Harbor's Rest, pay a fine, or you can come with me."
> 
> He looks at the black-hulled ship at the end of the pier — _The Marrow's Grace_.
> 
> "I'm looking for a crew. Not soldiers. Not slaves. People who've been pushed by the Iron Fleet and decided to push back. I sail honest and I don't take prisoners. You'd be the first to sign on. What do you say?"

**The pitch, and the (unspoken) hook:**

- He's **recruiting** — this is the "mysterious captain who saves the party" from the Free Captains faction.
- He's been **running from the Iron Fleet** for a long time. Rourke knows him by name. That's a thread to pull.
- He has a **compass at his throat** that doesn't quite belong — and he _whistled the Iron Fleet's march_ to get their attention. Small tells, no explanation yet.
- The ship is **black-hulled** and, if anyone looks closely, the wood seems to _move_ slightly when he's near. Don't call it out. Let them notice.

**If they ask — prepared answers (stay in character, don't over-explain):**

- **"Who are you?"** — "A captain. Name's Veyne." (He doesn't volunteer more.)
- **"Why me?"** — "Because you stood up when a man with a hammer told you to kneel. That's rarer than you'd think."
- **"What's that compass?"** — "A tool. For finding my way." (He touches it. It's warm.)
- **"Where are we going?"** — "Away. For now."

**The oath (the moment they commit):**

> "One rule. We don't betray each other. The tide takes what it wants, but we don't take from our own. Can you live with that?"

As he speaks, he touches the compass, and it **pulses once**. He looks at the players. *"The tide knows. That's all you need to know right now."*

If they say yes: **they're on the ship.**

## Scene 3.5 — Boarding the Marrow's Grace

**Read aloud (the ship):**

> The gangplank is worn smooth. The deck smells of salt and something older — iron, maybe. A single lantern burns blue-green in the rigging, and the wood of the hull seems to shift a little when you're not looking at it directly. 

**A small first task:** Elias asks them to help haul a line, or check a cargo hold. In the hold: crates marked with the Iron Fleet's gilded anchor. He doesn't comment. Let the players ask.

**The compass:** if anyone is near Elias, the compass at his throat is warm. Not hot. Warm. Like a living thing.

**The cliffhanger:** as the ship slips its moorings, the fog parts just enough for one player to see, on the far shore, the patrol ship's lamp still burning. **Rourke is still watching.** Elias notices. His jaw tightens.

> "He'll follow. Good. Let him see which way we sail."

The session ends as _The Marrow's Grace_ puts Harbor's Rest behind her and the fog closes in again. Elias stands at the helm, the compass at his throat faintly warm, and says — quietly, almost to himself —

> "Right. Now we sail."

## DM Prep — Before You Run This

**Pacing guide (≈60–70 min of play):**
- Scene 1, Tavern (15 min)
- Scene 1.5, Town walk (5 min)
- Scene 2, Docks (20–30 min)
- Rourke encounter (10 min)
- Elias's rescue (5 min)
- Scene 3, The oath (10 min)
- Scene 3.5, Boarding (5 min)

**What to prep:**
- The **tribute ledger** — one page of fake entries (names, "collections," dates).
- The **surrender cell** description, if the kneel path is taken.
- 2–3 answers to "Who is Veyne?" (use the box in Scene 3).

**What NOT to reveal (session 2+ material):**
- The compass's origin.
- What the _Marrow's Grace_ actually is.
- Elias's connection to the Free Captains.

**Next session hook:** Rourke's lamp on the far shore. Next session opens with the chase.
