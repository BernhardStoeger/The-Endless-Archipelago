### **1. The Bloodstained Logbook**

**Where to Find It:**

- Hidden in the ship’s **captain’s quarters** (locked in a drawer or behind a loose panel).
- Alternatively, found in the **ship’s cargo hold** among smuggled goods or stowed away by a loyal crew member.
- Could also be recovered from a **wrecked Iron Fleet ship** or a **Cult hideout**.

**What It Reveals:**

- The logbook belongs to **Lieutenant Harkin**, Elias’s former superior in the Iron Fleet.
- Entries detail Elias’s **undercover mission** infiltrating the Free Sails and his growing suspicions about the Iron Fleet’s true operations.
- A **final, frantic entry** describes Elias’s discovery of the **Cult’s sacrifice ritual** and his attempt to expose it. The last line reads:
    
    > _"He knows too much. The Fleet cannot allow this. Burn the evidence. The Tidecaller must be silenced."_
    
- The logbook ends abruptly, with **bloodstains on the final pages**, hinting at a violent confrontation.

**Implications:**

- Confirms Elias was **set up** by his own superiors.
- Suggests the Cult’s plans were **bigger than Elias’s crew**—they were using the Iron Fleet as a tool.
- Raises questions: _Why did Elias survive? Who saved him? And why does the crew still distrust him?_

### **2. The Crew’s Whispers in Port**

**Where to Find It:**

- Overheard in **taverns or dockside brothels** where the crew (or other Free Sail pirates) frequent.
- Spoken by a **fellow survivor** of the Iron Fleet’s betrayal, now hiding in a port city.
- Mentioned by a **Tideborn Monk** or a **rogue Iron Fleet deserter** who knew Elias in his past life.

**What It Revears:**

- Rumors paint Elias as a **traitor** who "sold out" his crew to the Cult. Some call him **"The Tidecaller’s Ghost"** or **"Half-Drowned."**
- A **specific incident** is often cited: During a mission, Elias **abandoned his post** to pursue a lead about the Cult, leaving his crew to be ambushed. (This was actually when he tried to warn them, but his warnings were ignored or dismissed.)
- A **ship’s cook or former crewmate** might admit:
    
    > _"Aye, he’s not right. One minute he’s leading us into battle, the next he’s muttering to the mast like it’s talking back. And don’t get me started on how he never leaves the ship."_
    
- A **Cult sympathizer** might taunt:
    
    > _"The Drowned God claims his due. Soon, the Tidecaller will belong to the waves."_
    

**Implications:**

- Explains why the crew **won’t trust Elias**, even if they’ve grown to respect him.
- Hints at the **ship’s sentience**—Elias isn’t just talking to the mast, he’s **communing with the ship itself**.
- Suggests the Cult’s influence is still **active in the Iron Fleet**, and Elias is a target.

### **3. The Drowned Wood Carvings**

**Where to Find It:**

- Etched into the **Marrow’s Grace’s hull** (visible during low tide or when the ship is beached).
- Carved into **hidden compartments** in the ship’s ribs or beneath the deck.
- Found on **drowned victims** washed ashore near Cult hideouts (their bodies bear similar carvings).

**What It Revears:**

- The carvings depict **symbols of the Drowned God**, but they are **incomplete or twisted**, as if someone tried to **ward off the Cult’s magic**.
- A **Tideborn Monk** might recognize them as **"the Mark of the Unclaimed Dead"**—a sign of those who were **saved by the divine entity but left forever changed**.
- If the party deciphers the carvings, they read like a **prayer or bargain**:
    
    > _"By wave and wood, I bind my fate.  
    > By blood and tide, I seal my weight.  
    > The Drowned God claims the lost,  
    > But I am bound to the Marrow’s cost."_
    
- The carvings **glow faintly** in Elias’s presence, as if reacting to his proximity.

**Implications:**

- Confirms Elias’s **connection to the divine entity** and the Cult’s ritual.
- Explains why the ship is **reinforced with drowned wood**—it’s a **protection (and a prison)**.
- Suggests Elias is **not the only one bound**—others might have made similar bargains.

### **4. The Iron Fleet Dispatch**

**Where to Find It:**

- Stolen from an **Iron Fleet captain’s quarters** during a raid.
- Found in the **safe of a corrupt Iron Fleet officer** who was later murdered.
- Hidden in a **Cult temple** as a warning to other would-be defectors.

**What It Revears:**

- A **classified dispatch** from the Iron Fleet’s high command, addressed to **"All Naval Units."**
- It orders the **capture or elimination of "The Tidecaller’s Crew"** for **"aiding the enemy"** (i.e., Elias).
- The dispatch mentions a **"divine intervention"** that **"corrupted" Elias** and **"must be undone."**
- A **postscript** from an unknown officer reads:
    
    > _"The Captain’s soul is now one with his vessel. The Drowned God’s work is incomplete, but we will finish it. Burn the ship. Drown the captain. The ritual is not yet done."_
    
- The dispatch is **sealed with the Iron Fleet’s sigil**, but the wax bears **drowned man symbols**—proof of Cult influence.

**Implications:**

- Confirms the **Cult’s obsession with Elias**—they see him as a **failed sacrifice** and want to **complete the ritual**.
- Explains why the Iron Fleet **hunts the Marrow’s Grace**: they believe Elias is still a threat to their plans.
- Raises the stakes: The party must **protect Elias** while also uncovering the truth about the Cult’s endgame.

### **5. Elias’s Waterlogged Journal**

**Where to Find It:**

- Hidden in the ship’s **navigational charts** (sealed in a waterproof pouch).
- Discovered in Elias’s **private cabin**, locked in a chest with a **tide-based lock** (only opens at high tide).
- Given to the party by a **dying Tideborn Monk** who helped Elias after the divine intervention.

**What It Revears:**

- A **fragmented personal journal** detailing Elias’s **transformation** and the moments leading up to the divine intervention.
- Entries describe:
    - His **discovery of the Cult’s plans** and his attempt to expose them.
    - The **night of the sacrifice**: His crew was **ambushed**, and he was **dragged to the altar**.
    - The **divine entity’s voice**: _"I can save them. But the cost is you."_
    - His **bargain**: He agreed, but the entity’s terms were **vague**—he didn’t realize he’d become bound to the ship.
- The final entry is **partially burned**, with a desperate note:
    
    > _"They call me a traitor. The crew won’t listen. The ship is my only witness. If I die, tell them the truth—_  
    > _I never left them. The Drowned God’s hands are still in this."_
    

**Implications:**

- Reveals the **full truth** of Elias’s betrayal: He **sacrificed himself to save his crew**, but no one believed him.
- Explains his **physical and emotional bond to the ship**—it’s his **only remaining connection to the world**.
- Sets up a **moral dilemma**: Should the party **tell the crew the truth**, risking their trust—or **let Elias suffer in silence**?

### **How to Weave the Clues into the Story**

1. **Start with Rumors**: Let the party overhear whispers in port (Clue 2) or find the logbook (Clue 1) early on. This plants doubt and curiosity.
2. **Uncover the Dispatch**: This might happen during a **heist or raid** on an Iron Fleet ship, raising the stakes.
3. **Reveal the Carvings**: During a **storm or when the ship is damaged**, the carvings become more pronounced, hinting at the ship’s sentience.
4. **Find the Journal**: This could be a **personal quest** for Elias, where the party must **prove their loyalty** to earn his trust.
5. **Confront Elias**: After gathering all clues, the party might **ask him directly**, forcing him to confess the truth—or worse, **watch as he weakens** when forced to leave the ship.
