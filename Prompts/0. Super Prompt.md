## Important Characters

### Captain Elias Veyne

#### Name: Captain Elias "Tidecaller" Veyne

#### Race: Half-Elf

#### Class: Ex-Intelligence Agent (formerly with the Iron Fleet) / Now Free Captain

#### Role: Captain of the Ship for which the players will be the crew. Mentor, Guide. 

#### Appearance:

- A weather-beaten man in his late 30s with sea-green eyes and silver-streaked dark hair, always braided with small shells and corals.
- Wears a patched-up coat and a compass necklace (a gift from the Tideborn Monks).
- Limps slightly from a gunshot wound (taken during a betrayal—see backstory).

#### Ship: The Marrow’s Grace

- A three-masted brigantine with reinforced hull (to withstand storms) and a hidden cargo hold (for smuggling or hiding refugees).
- Unique Features:
    - The mizzenmast is carved like a weeping willow, said to calm seas.
    - The deck planks are reinforced with drowned wood (rumored to repel the Cult’s magic).

#### Backstory:

Elias was once a high-ranking Intelligence Officer for the Iron Fleet, tasked with rooting out pirate networks. He infiltrated the Free Sails undercover, earning their trust—until he discovered the Iron Fleet was sabotaging ships to drown prisoners (a cover for the Cult’s rituals). When he tried to expose them, his superior ordered his execution.

- After almost drowning he was saved by a the [[The Tideborn Monks]] and now hunts the Iron Fleet’s corruption from the shadows.
- He won’t fight because his limp makes him slow in combat, and he relies on stealth, strategy, and allies.
- His guilt over his past actions (he once ordered the execution of innocents) drives him to protect the weak.

#### Personality:

- Charismatic but haunted. He tells stories of his past to bond with the crew but never glamorizes it.
- Strategic. Prefers hit-and-run tactics, ambushes, and outsmarting enemies.
- Protective. Will lie or bargain to keep the crew safe, even if it means breaking the Code of the Tides.

#### Why He Won’t Fight:

1. Physical Limitation: His limp makes him vulnerable in melee. He’s a master of navigational traps, sabotage, and tactical retreat.
2. Psychological: He refuses to be a weapon—his past as an enforcer disgusts him.
3. Moral: He believes true power is in unity, not brute force. He’d rather win by inspiring the crew than fighting himself.
4. Superstition: He thinks the Drowned God’s Cult targets those who spill blood unnecessarily.

#### Skills & Abilities:

- Expert Navigator: Can find hidden islands, read tides like a map, and avoid the Abyssal Maw.
- Persuasion & Deception: Can talk his way out of Iron Fleet raids or negotiate even with pirates.
- Tactical Leader: Plans complex heists, ambushes, and escapes.
- Minor Magic: Knows basic weather magic (gift from the Monks) to calm storms or create fog.
- Underdog Strength: Not a warrior, but his wits and leadership make him deadly in other ways.

#### Quirk:

- He whistles old Iron Fleet marches when nervous.
- He carries a waterlogged journal with names of Iron Fleet traitors and Cult sympathizers.
- He hates the color black (reminds him of the Drowned God’s Cult).

#### Why the Players Should Follow Him:

- He’s not a perfect leader—he makes mistakes, has secrets, and shows vulnerability. This makes him relatable.
- He respects the crew’s autonomy but will step in during crises.
- He has connections: Stormcallers, Monks, and even rogue Iron Fleet officers owe him favors.
- His ship is a haven for outcasts, and the players are part of that legacy.

# Factions

## Main Factions

### The Drowned God's Cult (Neutral Evil)

- Symbol: A drowned, skeletal hand clutching a trident, often found on cave walls or buried in tidal pools.
    
- Base of Operations: The Sunken Cathedral, a submerged temple complex near the Abyssal Maw, a whirlpool that leads to the depths. Their visible strongholds are on islands with black sands (where the ground is littered with petrified bones).
    
- Description: The cult worships Kraken’s Maw, a god-like entity sealed away after the Great Drowning (a cataclysmic flood that created most of the archipelago’s islands). They believe that if they awaken Kraken’s Maw, the world will be "purified" by drowning—with the faithful rising as Drowned Ones (undead servants) to rule the new watery world.
    
    - Methods:
        - They use necromancy to raise drowned sailors as husks.
        - They poison wells and sabotage ships to drown victims, creating more sacrifices.
        - They’ve infiltrated the Iron Fleet, planting agents who ensure that "unworthy" sailors are executed in ways that drown them.
    - Weakness: Their rituals require specific artifacts (e.g., the Crown of Tides, a relic from the Great Drowning) and blood sacrifices. Their numbers are small, but their influence is growing.
    
- Player Interaction: The players might stumble upon a drowned village, uncover a hidden ritual site, or be hired to stop a cultist from sinking a ship. The cult’s presence is subtle at first—missing sailors, strange tides, or fishermen who return... wrong.

### The Free Captains (Neutral Good)

- Nature: A loose confederation of captains who see themself as neutral, they are against the strict rules and taxes of the Iron Fleet as well as the tyrannical Methods of the Red Legion. They also are against the use of slaves used by the Iron Fleet and the Red Legion.
    
    > _"We answer to no chain but the tide, no law but the storm. We take what we need, share what we can, and drown the tyrants who would bind us."_
    
- Structure:
    - No Central Leadership: Captains are equals, but some have legendary reputations (e.g., the _"Stormcaller"_ who summons hurricanes, the _"Silent Tide"_ who navigates blind).
    - Their base is in the Whirlpool Archipelago, a group of smaller Islands surrounded by whirlpools, without knowledge of the right routes navigating through them is deadly.
    
- Player Hook:
    - The mysterious captain who saves the party is part of this faction. 


### The Iron Fleet (Lawful Neutral)

- Symbol: A gilded anchor with crossed swords.
    
- Base of Operations: The capital city of Veldros, located on a massive island called the Sovereign’s Spire. This island is dominated by a towering citadel where the High Marshal resides.
    
- Description: The Iron Fleet is the primary enforcer of law and order across the archipelago. They operate like a naval police force, collecting tributes from islands in exchange for protection from raiders, rebellions, and monsters. Their presence is both a shield and a shackle—islands that defy their rule risk being left vulnerable to pirates, storms, or worse.

    - Methods: They use large ships to patrol the waters and maintain order. Their soldiers are well-trained, heavily armored, and armed with standard weapons.

    - Corruption: While they claim to enforce justice, their heavy-handed tactics often lead to cruelty. Some islands pay tribute under threat, and the Iron Fleet’s Black Brigades (elite enforcers) are known for torturing dissenters.

    - Weakness: Their bureaucracy is slow to respond to local disputes, and their rule is resented by many. They also rely on proprietary navigation charts, which only they possess, making travel to remote islands difficult without their help.


- Player Interaction: If the players have ties to an Iron Fleet-occupied island, they might be recruited to root out rebellion or smuggling. Alternatively, they could be accused of crimes by the Iron Fleet (setting up a quest to clear their name or escape the law).


### The Pirate King's Red Legion (Lawful Evil)

- Symbol: A crowned skull with a dagger through its eye (symbolizing their rule and their betrayal).
    
- Base of Operations: Scarlet Isle, a volcanic island with blood-red sands. Their flagship, the Blood Harvest, is a fortress-ship.
    
- Description: The Pirate King, Captain Veyla Duskbane, is a ruthless and charismatic figure who believes the Iron Fleet’s rule is worse than chaos. She sees herself as a liberator, but her methods involve slavery, executions, and terror. She’s amassed a private navy of pirates who were cast out for cruelty.
    
    - Methods:
        - They raid Iron Fleet outposts, stealing supplies and freeing prisoners (who are often forced to join their crew).
    
    - Weakness: Despite their strength, they’re feared even by other pirates. Their brutality makes alliances difficult, and their volcanic island is a liability if the volcano erupts.
    
- Player Interaction: The Red Legion might attack the players’ ship or hire them to assassinate Iron Fleet officials in exchange for safe passage. They also will be a looming threat—the players hear rumors of their latest atrocity but don’t yet know where they’ll strike next.

## Smaller Factions
### The Tideborn Monks (Lawful Neutral)

- Symbol: A lotus flower floating on water.

- Base: The Veil Islands, a chain of mist-shrouded islands where the monks meditate in giant floating stupas.

- Description: Ascetics who believe the sea is a cycle of birth and death. They heal drowning victims (using magic to revive them) and warn of coming storms (via their "Tide Sight" powers). They despise the Drowned God’s Cult but won’t fight them directly.

- Interaction: The players might seek their help to raise a drowned ally or learn about the Cult’s plans.

### Pirates

- Symbol: Individual Pirates have their own symbol

- Description: Independents Pirates who steal and smuggle. Will sometimes work together but usually betrays each other
